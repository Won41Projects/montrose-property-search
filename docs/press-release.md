## The West End Loses an Estimated $25,000 Every Day to Out-of-Town Healthcare — A Local Hospital Would Keep It Here

**WEST END OF MONTROSE COUNTY, Colo.** — Every day, roughly **$25,000 leaves
the West End** — about $22,000 in Medicare, Medicaid, and insurance payments to
out-of-town hospitals, pharmacies, and labs, plus another $3,000 that families
spend out of their own pockets on fuel, meals, and lodging just to get there.
That adds up to more than **$9 million a year** lost from a community of 2,259
people — and by ⟨publication month⟩, more than **$⟨X⟩ million had already left
in 2026 alone**.

Most of that money is invisible to residents because it is paid by Medicare,
Medicaid, and private insurance directly to providers in Montrose, Grand
Junction, and beyond. But it is real money attached to the people who live
here — and right now, none of it stays.

The analysis counts only care a local facility would realistically provide —
emergency and urgent care, primary care visits, imaging, lab work, pharmacy,
and basic hospital stays. It deliberately excludes surgeries and specialty
care that would still be referred to larger hospitals, and it does not count
patients the hospital would draw from outside the district. The annual loss
breaks down to approximately:

- **$2.7–3.2 million** in pharmacy spending
- **$1.4–1.8 million** in emergency and urgent care
- **$1.2–1.5 million** in primary care and check-ups
- **$0.9–1.3 million** in imaging and lab work
- **$0.9–1.4 million** in basic hospital stays, observation, and therapy services
- **$0.9–1.5 million** in family travel costs (fuel, meals, lodging)

Keeping that care local would support **70–80 local jobs**, and research shows
every dollar a rural hospital spends generates about **$2.50 in local economic
activity** (University of Wyoming study). National research also shows the
stakes of doing nothing: a landmark University of North Carolina study found
that rural communities without their sole hospital suffer a **permanent 4%
decline in per-capita income** and a 1.6-point rise in unemployment.

There is also a human cost to distance. For a stroke, heart attack, or serious
injury, the chance of a good recovery falls steadily with every minute of
travel time — and today, West End residents face an hour or more each way, on
mountain roads, in every season.

The proposed **West End Health Service District** — covering Naturita, Nucla,
Redvale, Bedrock, and Paradox — will appear on the **November 2026** ballot.
The district's filed service plan describes a critical access hospital of
approximately 65,000 square feet with a preliminary project cost estimate of
$82.2 million, funded in part by a 20-mill levy on non-school assessed
property value (projected to generate $949,950 in 2027).

Residents can look up exactly what the proposed levy would cost their own
property — and compare it to what their household spends traveling for care —
at **https://montrose-property-search.onrender.com/**.

**Methodology:** Healthcare spending estimates are based on 2024 CMS National
Health Expenditure data ($15,474 per person in total health spending), CDC/NCHS
emergency department utilization rates, and AHRQ cost data, applied to the
district population of 2,259 stated in the filed WEHSD service plan. Travel
costs assume 2.5–3.5 out-of-town medical trips per resident per year at 100
miles each way, using the IRS $0.70/mile rate plus meals and occasional
overnight stays. Rural hospital economics: Holmes et al., *Health Services
Research*; Eilrich et al. (2015).
