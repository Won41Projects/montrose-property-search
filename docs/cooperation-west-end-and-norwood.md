# A hospital in Nucla and the clinic in Norwood

A starting point for how the two can work, and what each community gains. Not an agreement.

The West End Health Service District stops at the San Miguel County line. Naturita, Nucla, Redvale, Bedrock, and Paradox are in the mill. Norwood is not. Norwood does not pay it.

Basin Clinic is inside the district. When the hospital opens, that clinic’s place in Naturita becomes the hospital’s own clinic. Uncompahgre Medical Center stays independent in Norwood.

## Who keeps what

UMC keeps primary care and the medical home, dental care, behavioral health, and its own pharmacy.

The hospital keeps the emergency room, day and night. It keeps inpatient beds and observation, a swing bed so someone can recover here and go home, and hospital imaging and lab.

A patient who needs a bed goes to the hospital. When they are ready, they go back to the doctor, dentist, and pharmacy they already have. Each pharmacy stays with its own clinic.

Other Colorado towns already live with this split. In Lamar, Craig, and Estes Park, the community health center kept dental, behavioral health, and pharmacy. The small hospital kept the emergency room, the beds, and the imaging. Those hospitals also run a family clinic. That is the overlap to settle here. This hospital replaces Basin Clinic’s daytime practice. It should not try to become Norwood’s medical home.

## Why the money can sit side by side

Medicare pays a clinic a set amount for an office visit. In 2026 that base is about $208, and Medicare pays 80 percent of it. Medicare pays a critical access hospital about 101 percent of its allowable cost for the emergency visit, the stay, and the swing bed. A clinic cannot rebill an office visit as a hospital stay. A hospital does not take a dental chair or a sliding-fee practice. The dollars are for different work.

## What the West End gains

Residents get a local emergency room, a bed, and imaging. They do not get a second dental office or a second pharmacy. The office care Basin provides today continues inside the hospital. The levy they pay is for that hospital. It is not a tax on Norwood.

From the West End, Montrose or Grand Junction is close to 2 hours, and those roads can be treacherous at night or in weather. The hospital puts that care inside the community.

## What Norwood gains

Norwood pays no mill and keeps the full clinic: primary care, dental, behavioral health, and pharmacy.

Residents gain a hospital 20 to 30 minutes away, on the road that usually stays ordinary, instead of about 90 minutes to Montrose. People who skip Norwood today, because they expect to be sent on to Montrose anyway, have a reason to start at UMC and come back there.

UMC does not lose a bill it can send today. The trip to Montrose was never UMC’s revenue. A closer hospital makes the Norwood medical home more useful, because the backup is no longer a long, weather-prone drive.
