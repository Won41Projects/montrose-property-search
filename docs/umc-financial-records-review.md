# Uncompahgre Medical Center financial records review

Reference note. Uncompahgre Medical Center’s public financials are consistent. The audited balance sheet and the Form 990 match to the dollar, and the gap between the two “surplus” headlines is explained by two presentation choices.

The clinic is Uncompahgre Combined Clinics, doing business as Uncompahgre Medical Center, the federally qualified health center in Norwood (EIN 84-1071822). Its year ends January 31. CliftonLarsonAllen’s audit for the year ended January 31, 2025, dated October 22, 2025, gives an **unmodified opinion** on the financial statements. The same report gives a **qualified opinion** on the federal Health Center Program because sliding-fee discounts were applied incorrectly.

Revenue was about one-third patient services ($2,152,174), one-third pharmacy ($1,946,161), and one-third grants ($2,134,727). Donations and other income were $72,864. About 68 percent of operating revenue came from government health programs or government grants, down from 74 percent the year before.

The surplus fell from $605,625 the year before because grant revenue dropped $647,173, from $2,781,900 to $2,134,727. Patient and pharmacy revenue rose $448,006. Expenses rose $202,902. There is no drawn debt. Net assets are entirely unrestricted.

Federal award spending in FY2025 was $1,634,161, of which $1,589,608 was the Health Center Program cluster. The rest of grant revenue is state and local.

## Why the surplus figures differ by $22,610

Form 990 Part XI reports an unrealized investment gain of $22,610 outside revenue. Adding it to the 990 surplus of $207,537 equals the audited surplus of $230,147. The audit includes that gain in “other” operating revenue of $49,633. Interest and miscellaneous income on the 990 ($20,213 and $6,810) plus the unrealized gain equal that $49,633 line. The same pattern in the prior year is a $15,349 unrealized gain.

The revenue lines differ by $122,453, which is the sliding-fee gross-up of $145,063 minus this $22,610 gain kept out of 990 revenue.

## Why revenue and expenses are each about $145,000 higher on the 990

The audit nets sliding-fee discounts against revenue and describes them as approximately $145,000. The 990 reports patient revenue of $2,260,195 and pharmacy revenue of $1,983,203, together $145,063 above the audited patient and pharmacy lines, and reports expenses higher by the same $145,063. The net result is unchanged. In the prior year that gross-up was $205,362, matching the audit’s “approximately $205,000.”

## Receivables

Patient receivables fell from $375,584 to $264,032 even as patient revenue rose, about 24 days of patient and pharmacy revenue. The mix shifted toward commercial payors: Medicare 18 percent and Medicaid 14 percent of receivables, other third parties 66 percent, and patients 2 percent. Management recorded no allowance for credit losses. The balance is 6.7 percent of assets, and the auditor accepted the estimate. Medicare settlements were described as not significant.

## The qualified finding

On the Health Center grant (Assistance Listing 93.224 and 93.527), 7 of 60 tested reduced-charge visits had the wrong discount or a discount without support. The prior year was 5 of 40. Questioned costs were none, and the auditors found no material weakness in controls over the financial statements. That control report is not an opinion that controls are effective. The clinic is not a low-risk auditee.

For the second year, the auditors found a material weakness in controls over the sliding-fee schedule required of a health center. About one in eight tested reduced-charge visits got the wrong discount or a discount that was not supported. Management agreed and described a new verification process; that response was not audited. The charity-care note depends on this same process, so patient-level discounts are less reliable than the clinic-wide totals, which still tie.

## One classification that changes how the cash balance reads

The audit reports cash and cash equivalents of $2,066,110. The 990 splits that exact total into bank cash of $1,599,039, savings of $14,128, and publicly traded securities of $452,943. The prior year splits the same way and also totals to the audited cash line. Those securities rose from $313,950 and produced the $22,610 unrealized gain, about a 7 percent mark-to-market move. That return is the profile of a brokerage portfolio, not a money-market deposit. The audit still classifies the whole amount as cash, and the cash-flow statement shows no separate purchase of investments, which follows from that classification.

Liquidity is real. Current assets cover current liabilities about six times, and an unused margin line of $175,584 at 8.75 percent was undrawn. Of the reported cash, $452,943 can change in value.
