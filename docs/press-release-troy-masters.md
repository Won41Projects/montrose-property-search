## Masters: "I Ran the Numbers — the West End Loses $25,000 Every Day Without a Hospital"

**NUCLA, Colo.** — Troy Masters, a candidate for the board of the proposed
West End Health Service District on this November's ballot, says the question
facing voters isn't whether the West End can afford a hospital — it's whether
it can keep affording not to have one.

"Every single day, about $25,000 leaves this community," Masters said.
"Roughly $22,000 of it is Medicare, Medicaid, and insurance money paid to
hospitals, pharmacies, and labs in Montrose and Grand Junction for everyday
care — X-rays, check-ups, prescriptions, emergency visits. The other $3,000
is what our families spend out of their own pockets on gas, meals, and motel
rooms just to get there. We never see that money leave, because the insurance
companies pay it directly. But it's our money, attached to our people, and
right now none of it stays."

Masters, ⟨brief bio: years in the West End, occupation, family/community
ties⟩, didn't take anyone's word for the numbers — he built a public
calculator to check them. The tool, available at
**montrose-property-search.onrender.com**, lets any property owner look up
their actual parcel and see exactly what the proposed 20-mill levy would cost
them — senior exemption included — side by side with what their household
spends traveling for care.

"I built it because I think voters deserve the real number for their own
property, not a slogan," Masters said. "For most households, the travel they're
already paying for costs more than the levy will. Run your own address and
see."

The outflow adds up to an estimated **$8 to $10.6 million every year** from
the district's 2,259 residents — the population stated in the district's filed
service plan. The analysis counts only care a local critical access hospital
would actually provide — emergency and urgent care, primary care, imaging,
labs, pharmacy, and basic hospital stays — and deliberately excludes the
surgeries and specialty care that would still be referred to larger hospitals.
Approximate annual breakdown:

- **$2.7–3.2 million** in pharmacy spending
- **$1.4–1.8 million** in emergency and urgent care
- **$1.2–1.5 million** in primary care and check-ups
- **$0.9–1.3 million** in imaging and lab work
- **$0.9–1.4 million** in basic hospital stays, observation, and therapy services
- **$0.9–1.5 million** in family travel costs (fuel, meals, lodging)

Keeping that care local would support **70–80 local jobs**, and research shows
every dollar a rural hospital spends generates about **$2.50 in local economic
activity** (University of Wyoming study). The stakes of doing nothing are also
documented: a University of North Carolina study found that rural communities
without their sole hospital suffer a permanent **4% decline in per-capita
income** and higher unemployment.

"My grandparents' generation built the roads, the schools, and the power that
made it possible to live here," Masters said. "This is our generation's
version of that decision. And for our elders, it's personal — right now, when
someone needs rehab or long-term care, we send them over the divide and visit
when we can. A hospital with swing beds and long-term care means they stay
home, with us, where they belong."

The proposed district — covering Naturita, Nucla, Redvale, Bedrock, and
Paradox — will appear on the **November 2026** ballot, along with candidates
for its first elected five-member board. The filed service plan describes a
critical access hospital of approximately 65,000 square feet with a
preliminary project cost estimate of $82.2 million, funded in part by the
20-mill levy (projected to generate $949,950 in 2027).

As a board candidate, Masters says his commitment is transparency: "An elected
board, public budgets, annual audits — and my promise that every number we put
in front of voters will trace to a source you can check. That's how I built
the calculator, and that's how I'd serve on this board. If my neighbors find
an error in my math, I want to hear about it — that's the point of showing
your work."

Voters can verify the figures, look up their own property, and see the running
total of healthcare dollars lost this year at
**montrose-property-search.onrender.com**.

**Methodology:** Healthcare spending estimates are based on 2024 CMS National
Health Expenditure data, CDC/NCHS emergency department utilization rates, and
AHRQ cost data, applied to the district population of 2,259 stated in the
filed WEHSD service plan. Travel costs assume 2.5–3.5 out-of-town medical
trips per resident per year at 100 miles each way, using the IRS $0.70/mile
rate plus meals and occasional overnight stays. Rural hospital economics:
Holmes et al., *Health Services Research*; Eilrich et al. (2015). Jobs figure
(70–80) from the district's filed materials.
